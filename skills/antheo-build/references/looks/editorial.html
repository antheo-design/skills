Timetables people can read at a glance

The ferry company’s printed timetable had 1,140 numbers on one page. We rebuilt it around the three questions people ask at a pier.

By Ruth Okonjo · 12 November 2026 · 6 minutes

Most people at a ferry pier want one of three things: when the next boat leaves, when the last one back leaves, and whether today is different. The old timetable answered all three, but only to someone willing to read a grid of six routes, two seasons and a column of footnotes.

We spent two mornings on the piers at Kirkwall and Stromness with a clipboard. Of 214 people we watched, 162 looked at the timetable for under ten seconds, and most of them then asked a member of staff anyway.

One route, one day, one page

The new timetable shows one route at a time, for today, in the order the boats leave. Tomorrow is a tap away; the season’s full grid is still there for the few who plan a month ahead.

The last boat back is set apart at the foot of the list, because it was the question with the highest stakes: miss it and you are staying the night.

We stopped asking how to fit every crossing on a page, and asked what someone on the pier needs before the boat leaves.

Ruth Okonjo, design lead

What changed on the pier

In the four weeks after the new boards went up, questions to staff about departure times fell from about 90 a day to about 35. The printed grid is still on the wall. Fewer people read it.

Two things did not work. The first board put the next departure in red, which looked like a delay; it is now in the text colour, larger. And the countdown in minutes confused people when a boat was held, so it now shows the time instead.
